# F-095 — UC: the ace lock holds on the server, not only in the app

Created: 2026-10-03 · Implemented: 2026-10-04 · Status: implemented

## The gap
Undercut (root app, the Pit Wall portal, the Firestore rules and the lock sweep).

The ace doubles a driver's or constructor's points. It locks at **race start** — later than the
roster, which locks at qualifying — and that later lock existed **only in the client**:

| | locks when | enforced by |
|---|---|---|
| roster (drivers, constructor, budget) | qualifying | `isLocked`, the rules' denied-field list, the team callables |
| **ace** | **race start** | **`lockout.ts:102` in the app, and nothing else** |

- `firestore.rules` denied a named list of fields. **`aceDriverId` and `aceConstructorId` were not
  on it**, and `isLocked` was protected *from being changed* rather than consulted as a condition.
- `web/pitwall/src/lib/teamApi.ts` writes the ace with a direct `updateDoc`; the app writes it
  through `syncTeamToFirebase` after an equally client-side check.
- `functions/src/scoring/calculatePoints.ts:318` reads `team.aceDriverId` **live** when it scores,
  and re-checks only `ACE_MAX_PRICE`. It never asked whether the race had started.

**So: between lights out and the moment scoring ran, a player could set their ace to whoever was
winning and have those points doubled.** That window is hours wide and needs no exploit beyond the
Firebase web SDK and the player's own credentials.

## Why the obvious fix was wrong
A one-line rule denying ace changes while `isLocked == true` was written and reverted before it
reached production. It would have broken a designed feature: `isLocked` is the *qualifying* lock,
and moving the ace after seeing qualifying — right up to lights out — is deliberate.
`lockout.ts:101-102` says so in two lines:

```
isNaturallyLocked    = now >= lockTime        (qualifying)
isAceNaturallyLocked = now >= raceStartTime   (race start)
```

Any fix has to distinguish the two locks, and the rules cannot read a race start time out of the
race document without a `get()` the team doc gives them no key for.

## What was built
**The deadline is stamped on the team, so the rules can compare it to `request.time`.** No `get()`,
no second collection, and exact to the second rather than rounded up to the next sweep.

1. **`functions/src/locks/teamLocks.ts`** — `autoLockTeams` already writes
   `lockStatus.nextUnlockTime` from `race.schedule.race` at the qualifying lock. It now also writes
   **`lockStatus.aceLockTime: race.schedule.race`**. `autoUnlockTeams` and `earlyUnlockTeam` clear
   it. `teamOperations.ts` creates teams with the field present and null.
2. **`firestore.rules`** — a new `aceDeadlinePassed(team)` helper, and an update clause denying any
   diff touching `aceDriverId` or `aceConstructorId` once it is true. `lockStatus` is already a
   denied key, so the deadline is not the client's to move.
3. **`src/utils/lockout.ts`** — `serverAceLocked(lockStatus, isLocked, now)`, mirroring the rule.
   The app's `GridTeamPanel` and all three store mutations (`setAce`, `setAceConstructor`,
   `clearAce`) take either it or the calendar-derived lock.
4. **`web/pitwall/src/data/team.ts`** — `aceFrozen(team, now)`, the same predicate, wired into the
   ace gate in `state.tsx` and the Compare card's button.

### Two deliberate choices in that
**The rule only looks at the deadline while the team is locked.** Between races `aceLockTime` can
still hold the last race's start, which is in the past for ever; consulting it on an unlocked team
would freeze the ace permanently if any unlock path ever missed the clear. The clears are there as
well — stale state is how the next bug starts — but the rule does not depend on them.

**The Pit Wall portal gets *less* strict, not more.** It was gating the ace on `isLocked`, which is
the qualifying lock, so it refused every ace move from Saturday onward and threw away the window
the game deliberately gives you. It now refuses exactly when the server does. The Compare button
reads "The race has started" instead of "Locked for this weekend", and a bare `permission-denied`
from the direct ace write — the only step in `executePlan` that is not a callable, and so the only
one with no sentence of its own — is translated instead of surfacing as
"The server refused this step:".

## Why the scorer was left reading live
`calculatePoints` still reads `team.aceDriverId` at scoring time rather than from a frozen
snapshot. With the rules enforcing to the second there is no longer a window in which that value
can change after the race starts, and freezing the ace into `raceSnapshots` would mean reworking
the scoring path for a hole that is now closed. The sturdier version is still the better long-term
shape if any other field ever wants a race-start lock.

## Tests
- `rules-tests/firestore.rules.test.js` — five cases against the emulator: the quali-to-lights-out
  window still works; the ace is frozen after the deadline (driver, constructor, clear, and riding
  along with an innocent write); re-writing the *same* ace is not a change and is not blocked;
  a stale deadline on an unlocked team frees nothing; documents written before the field existed
  are unaffected; and the deadline itself cannot be moved by the owner. **Verified to fail against
  the previous rules file** — reverting `firestore.rules` fails exactly the freeze case.
- `__tests__/store/aceLockoutCalendar.test.ts` — three new cases, including the one that matters:
  five hours after the race the calendar has moved on to the next round and reports the ace free,
  while the team is still locked and still carries the deadline. **Verified to fail without the
  store guard.** Also the symmetric case: a stamp still in the future does not lock.
- `__tests__/utils/lockout.test.ts` and `web/pitwall/src/data/team.test.ts` — the two predicates,
  including every shape the timestamp arrives in (Firestore `Timestamp`, the admin twin, the
  `{seconds}` object a persisted store rehydrates, ISO string, epoch ms) and the fail-open
  behaviour on an unreadable one.

## Deploy order
**Functions before rules.** The rule is inert until something stamps a deadline, so deploying it
first changes nothing; deploying functions first means the next lock sweep stamps teams that the
old rules do not yet consult, which is also harmless. Either order is safe — the pair only has to
be in place before the next qualifying. The app and portal changes ride their own releases; an
installed 2.4.2 client with no knowledge of the field simply sees its ace write refused and logs
it, which is the status quo for any denied write.

## Still open
- **A team created after the lock sweep is never locked for that weekend at all.** `autoLockTeams`
  queries `isLocked == false` once, an hour before qualifying; a team made after that has
  `isLocked: false` through the race, so the roster callables (`teamOperations.ts:69` gates on
  `team.isLocked`) accept edits during the race and this ace deadline is never stamped. Wider than
  the ace and not caused by this change: the roster is the bigger half. Self-penalising today —
  the player would have to abandon an existing team and its points — but it wants its own feature.
- The scorer still reads the ace live (above).

## Evidence of the original gap
- `firestore.rules` — `fantasyTeams` update rule, denied-field list without the ace fields
- `web/pitwall/src/lib/teamApi.ts:62` — direct `updateDoc({ aceDriverId })`
- `src/services/team.service.ts:347` — `if (!team.lockStatus.canModify) throw` (client-side)
- `src/utils/lockout.ts:101-102` — the two different lock times
- `functions/src/scoring/calculatePoints.ts:318-325` — live read, cap re-checked, lock not
- `functions/src/locks/teamLocks.ts:126` — where the race-start stamp now goes

## Severity
A player could inflate their own score in a league others play honestly, and the Pit Wall Pass
makes that a paid product sitting next to an unfair ladder. No data was exposed and no other
account was reachable; the harm was competitive integrity.
