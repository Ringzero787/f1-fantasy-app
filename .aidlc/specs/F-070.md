# F-070 — UC: Pit Wall projections and price model

Created: 2026-09-19 · Status: building

## Goal
Undercut (root app). Every recommendation in the portal rests on a number the user can trust: what each driver and constructor is likely to score next race under Undercut's real scoring, how wide the range is, and what it does to their price. Statistical model, not an LLM.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Inputs are limited to our own game data and race classifications (`raceScores`, `races`, prices, circuit characteristics we maintain), plus one external feed for conditions only: the MET Norway session forecast (NLOD / CC BY 4.0, commercial use permitted, attributed on the page), which feeds no projection. Projections are a pass feature, so they must not consume timing-derived aggregates (ADR-001); a test asserts the model's input set.
- [ ] Runs as a job on the forge worker (F-069), importing `scoringCore` and the price rules from `functions/src` directly.
- [ ] Per round, a Monte Carlo simulation (>= 10,000 runs) produces for every driver and constructor: floor (15th pct), median, ceiling (85th pct), points split (qualifying, race position, positions gained, sprint, fastest lap, DNF), model probabilities for win / podium / top 10 / DNF, and ace-doubled values. Teammate and constructor correlation and safety-car variance are modelled.
- [ ] Scoring comes from the shared `scoringCore` so projections and real scoring cannot drift (test asserts identical points for a fixture result).
- [ ] Projections refresh after each practice and qualifying session; the history of each refresh is kept so the portal can chart projection movement across the weekend.
- [ ] Price model: for each asset, the points thresholds for each price step, P(rise), P(fall) and expected change, derived from the real rules in `functions/src/pricing/updatePrices.ts`.
- [ ] Derived metrics: value (points per $), plus/minus vs price-implied points, consistency (% of races beating expectation), hit rates for user-adjustable thresholds (L5/L10/season), rolling DNF hazard, circuit-fit score per car per upcoming round.
- [ ] Backtest report over every scored 2025-2026 race: mean absolute error, calibration of the floor/ceiling band (target 65-75% of outcomes inside), and price-direction accuracy. Published in the portal footer; the model does not ship if it fails to beat a "last 3 races average" baseline.
- [ ] Probabilities are presented as model estimates. No bookmaker odds, no odds formats, no wagering language anywhere.

### Publishing and reading the payloads

- [ ] The job publishes two documents per round with the shape the portal already renders: `pw_pages/{season}_{round}` for pass holders and `pw_public/{season}_{round}` for the free look. The free one is built field by field, never spread from the full one, so a paid field added later is not inherited into the free look the day it ships; a test adds to the full payload and asserts the free one does not follow.
- [ ] The free document lists every driver and constructor with identity and price, and withholds the projections past the top ten. Identity and price are not what the pass sells, and the portal needs the whole grid to edit a lineup, which is free.
- [ ] The portal reads the newest published document for the viewer's entitlement, ordered by `asOf` rather than document id, and falls back to the browser-generated example set when nothing is published or the rules refuse the read. Every field is coerced on the way in, so a document from an older or newer worker still renders.
- [ ] A payload is tagged with the access it was fetched for, so a pass that lapses or a sign-out cannot leave paid content on screen while the next read is in flight.
- [ ] The pipeline lands one piece at a time, so the portal can tell "published as zero" from "not published at all" and each frame says which. A frame with no source may not draw a shape from the row order: no leverage column built from a zero ownership, no circuit-fit claim from a flat neutral, no qualifying gap that puts the whole grid on pole. Every check errs towards "not published".
- [ ] The scroll budget run measures every page again with only what the worker publishes today, so the "not published yet" states are proven to fit and to render without a page error.
- [ ] The price model reaches the portal as published numbers, not as an approximation derived from the price in the browser: points to rise, points to avoid a fall, and both chances.

### The session forecast and the forecast map

- [ ] Session weather comes from MET Norway Locationforecast (NLOD / CC BY 4.0), attributed on the tile, requested with an identifying User-Agent, and reported as what the source measures: sky, temperature and millimetres of rain in the session's hour. No probability is invented from an amount.
- [ ] The forecast map is a grid of the same point forecasts around the circuit (5 x 5, 40 km apart) drawn by the portal, not an embedded map service: no third-party scripts or tracking in the portal, and a licence that permits commercial use. Per session within nine days it carries frames at -3 h, the session and +3 h, rain per cell and wind at the centre, so a band of rain can be seen approaching.
- [ ] Grid cells are published row-major from the north-west corner and the portal draws index 0 at the top left; a test pins the first offset to (west, north) so the map cannot be published upside down.
- [ ] Every request to the forecast service is bounded by a timeout, a cell that fails is drawn as unknown rather than dry, and the job continues without the map when the service is unavailable.
- [ ] The portal validates each frame's cell count against the published radius and drops any frame that would draw a scrambled grid; a map with no drawable frame is "not published", and the weather tile says so beneath the session rows rather than staying silent.

### The wire (first cut, in place of F-071's pipeline until it lands)

- [ ] Headlines come from the app's own `articles` collection (the official feeds the app already gathers), read by the job outside the model's input allowlist, so the allowlist stays truthful and no timing-derived aggregate reaches the free document.
- [ ] Each headline is tagged with the driver or constructor it names (a driver beats a team; a team is recognised by its short and full name, never by a sponsor or a generic word like "team" or "racing"), a kind (penalty, regulation, contract, practice, qualifying, race, news) and a tone; general stories about nobody on the grid are dropped.
- [ ] Only an `https` link is published, and the portal checks the scheme again before rendering an anchor; links open in a new tab with `rel="noopener"`.
- [ ] Headline and link are free; the body rides only in the paid document (ADR-001).
