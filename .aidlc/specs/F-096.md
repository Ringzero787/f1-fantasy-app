# F-096 — PW: recommendations carry confidence, not just a delta

Created: 2026-10-04 · Status: building

## Goal
Pit Wall (web portal). A reader followed the Briefing's recommendations and watched their weekend
score potential fall from 298 to 264.

Nothing in the list was lying about its own number. Each swap really did project higher, each card
really did state a true fact. The fault is that every card stated **one** dimension and hid the
rest, and the list ranked on the median alone — so it compared a driver at the sharp end against a
driver in the midfield as though a projected point meant the same thing in both places.

The shape of the fix comes from the Track Limits GP model, which splits the grid into variance zones
and prices the midfield as a coin flip: *"16 drivers fight for 14 finishing positions and chaos
dominates pace differences."* Its σ values are deliberately **not** borrowed — they are that model's
own 2025 backtest on a different system, and importing a number someone else calibrated would claim
a confidence we have not earned. The weight here comes from the floor-to-ceiling band this payload
already publishes.

## Acceptance criteria
- [x] **A projection carries a confidence.** `confidenceOf` returns a zone, the published band, and
      a weight in 0..1 derived as `1 / (1 + spread/median)` — dimensionless, monotone, and never
      zero, because a wide projection is worth less rather than nothing.
- [x] The zone comes from the chance of a top-ten finish, which is the signal this payload has for
      "front, midfield, or back": at or above 75 is the sharp end, at or below 25 is compressed, and
      between them is the coin flip. A constructor reads as front — it is two drivers summed, so it
      moves less than either. **The zone is cosmetic**: it names the grid position for a reader and
      never enters the weight, which comes from the band alone. The two are reported separately
      (`label` and `rangeLabel`) after a first draft conflated them into "their range is tight (27
      points between floor and ceiling)".
- [x] **Swaps are ordered by edge, not by raw gain.** `edge = gain × weight(incoming)`: the gain is
      only as good as the pick that has to deliver it. The raw gain is still what the card shows,
      because that is what the reader is being offered.
- [x] **The RISK card states what its swap costs and withholds the one-click when it costs points.**
      `nearest()` chooses on price and retirement risk and knows nothing about projection, so the
      swap it finds could cost a great deal — and it was offered as a one-click anyway, under copy
      claiming "a safer floor" that the code never checked (it filters on `dnf`). The card keeps its
      place, because knowing your riskiest driver is useful on its own; it may no longer hand over a
      downgrade in one click.
- [x] **The band is a compared row, not decoration.** Floor and ceiling were already shown and never
      subtracted. `Range width` is the one row where the smaller number wins.
- [x] **The caveat sits with the recommendations**, not in a footer: the ordering is a lean derived
      from our own published band, not a calibrated variance model, and the list says so where the
      reader is deciding.
- [x] Every part is pure and unit-tested with no browser: the confidence model, the ranking, the
      RISK card's two branches, and the new compare row.

## Targets
- web (Pit Wall portal)

## Data
No new data, no new payload field, nothing new transmitted. `confidenceOf` reads `med`, `floor`,
`ceil` and `t10`, all already published and all already on screen. The weight is computed in the
browser from them.

## Performance budget
No new dependency and no new call. One arithmetic pass per candidate in a list that is already
sorted; the swap search was O(lineup × market) before and still is.

## Out of scope
- **Calibrating the weight against outcomes.** The right version measures how often a projection of
  a given band width lands where it said, over a season, and derives the discount from that — which
  is what the Track Limits model does with its σ, and what would let this stop calling itself a lean.
  It needs a backtest this portal does not have yet.
- **Probability and fair-odds presentation.** The source model pairs every line with a probability
  and a price (`Under 4.5 · 48% · +107`). The equivalent here would be "this swap gains 6 points,
  with a 60% chance of gaining anything at all", which needs the distribution the weight only
  approximates.
- **Calibrating against outcomes**, as above — that is the only thing here that is genuinely out of
  scope rather than simply done.

Note what is *not* out of scope, because the first draft of this spec got it wrong: the **Lineup
Lab** renders "Recommended moves" from the same `swapRecs`, so it is reordered by edge too. That is
the intended behaviour — one ranking rule, not two — but it means the Lab shows an edge-ordered list
without the caveat that sits beside the Briefing's. Worth a line there.

## Known gaps
- **The weight is a judgement, not a measurement.** A band twice as wide halves a point because that
  is a defensible reading of the band, not because it was fitted. The caveat on screen says so, and
  the out-of-scope note above is the way to earn a stronger claim.
- **`t10` is a proxy for grid position.** It separates the three zones well at the ends and is
  fuzziest exactly where the midfield boundary sits. A published predicted finish would be better.
- **On the free payload the weight does nothing.** `floor`, `ceil` and `t10` are published as zero
  outside the pass, so every weight is 1 and the ordering quietly reverts to raw gain. That is the
  right failure — it cannot invent confidence it has not been given — but it is silent, and the
  range reads as "unpublished" rather than claiming a number.
- **Not localized**, like the rest of the portal.
