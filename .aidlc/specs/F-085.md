# F-085 — UC: Revoke a pass when the store refunds it

Created: 2026-10-02 · Status: building

## Goal
Undercut (root app, functions). Every store's refund signal was read exactly once, at the moment of purchase, and never again. A buyer could take the Pit Wall Pass, refund it through the store, and keep access until the season ends in January. Stripe was the only purchase route with a revocation path, through its webhook.

This re-checks live pass purchases against the store that sold them and takes the pass back when the store says the money went back.

## Why a sweep and not a webhook
Push notifications from three stores mean three consoles configured, a Pub/Sub topic and an endpoint per store, each of which can be misconfigured silently. We have just spent a day on a Play permission that was granted to the wrong account and looked correct in the console, and on a documentation line that sent it there. A scheduled re-check needs none of that configuration and fails visibly in one log line.

A season pass can tolerate a day of latency. It cannot tolerate a revocation path nobody can tell is broken.

## Acceptance criteria
- [x] A scheduled job re-checks every `purchases` document for `pitwall.pass.season` with `status: validated`, daily.
- [x] **A pass is only ever taken away on a definite refund.** A store that times out, a response in a shape we do not recognise, a missing field, a purchase that cannot be re-checked at all — every one of those leaves the pass alone. The verdict type has three values and only `refunded` revokes.
- [x] Google Play: `purchaseState` of 1 is a refund. 0 and 2 are not; pending is money not yet taken, which is neither a sale nor a refund.
- [x] Amazon: a `cancelDate` is a refund. Amazon sends milliseconds, so zero means not cancelled rather than cancelled at the epoch. A response without the field at all is unknown.
- [x] Apple legacy receipts: a cancellation on **this** product is a refund. A refund of a different product in the same receipt is not.
- [x] **Only the pass that purchase paid for is taken.** A refund is about one transaction, and the buyer may have purchased again since. `grantPass` records the granting purchase in `pass.ref`, and the sweep revokes only when that matches the document being refunded. A superseded purchase is recorded as refunded, left alone, and logged with the pass that replaced it.
- [x] The pass is revoked **before** the record is marked. The other order drops the document out of the query forever, so a failed revoke would leave a refunded pass alive with nothing to surface it.
- [x] One document that throws cannot end the sweep, and a non-zero failure count is logged at error level rather than buried in a summary.
- [x] No throwable is logged whole: the Amazon shared secret rides in that request's URL.
- [x] The decision logic is pure and tested without a network, with most of the tests about refusing to revoke.

## Targets
- android
- ios
- amazon

## Data
Reads `purchases` (store tokens and receipts already stored at purchase) and writes `users/{uid}.pass` and `pwRevoked` through `revokePass`, which also clears the league Pro flags and the grant records. No new personal data is collected or stored. The stored token is sent only to the store that issued it.

## Performance budget
One scheduled run a day over every validated pass purchase. Deliberately not paginated or cursored: the number of pass holders is small enough that checking all of them is cheaper than a cursor that could silently skip someone.

## Out of scope, and honestly so
- **StoreKit 2 purchases on iOS cannot be re-checked this way at all.** A signed transaction is a fixed artefact: re-verifying the copy we stored returns exactly what it returned the first time, refund or not. Every 2.4.0 iOS purchase is StoreKit 2, so in practice this covers Android and Amazon today and the legacy Apple path that almost nobody is on. `checkable()` says so rather than quietly reporting those as active.
- Closing that gap needs App Store Server Notifications V2 or the App Store Server API, which needs its own key, its own endpoint and its own feature.
- Refunding a purchase we never granted, and partial refunds.
