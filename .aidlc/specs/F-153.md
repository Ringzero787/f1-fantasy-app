# F-153 — UC: Pit Wall worker alert emails in plain words

Created: 2026-11-17 · Status: planned

## Goal

On 2026-11-17 the box lost its internet connection from about 10:19 to 10:55 UTC. The worker's
watchdog (F-132) did its job: the stuck tick was given up on, two more refused to start beside it,
the process exited and systemd brought it back. The owner's only notice was one email whose whole
body was `Error: previous tick still running` and a stack frame. It did not say what had happened,
whether anything was broken, whether players were affected or whether to do anything, and nothing
ever said the worker had recovered. The owner had to ask a session to find out it was a non-event.

Every email the worker sends now reads, in order: what happened, what the worker does about it by
itself, what the reader needs to do (usually nothing), what players see, and only then the
technical detail. The first normal tick after a long silence sends an "is back" email, so a stall
email is never the last word.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `workers/pitwall/src/jobs/alertText.ts` is pure (no Firestore, no network) and builds the subject and text for a failed tick, a failed job, stale published data and the all-clear; pinned in `workers/pitwall/test/alertText.test.js`.
- [ ] A failed tick is sorted into deadline / still-stalled / network / other from its message; the subject names which and carries "n of 3"; the text has WHAT HAPPENED, WHAT THE WORKER DOES ABOUT IT, WHAT YOU NEED TO DO, WHAT PLAYERS SEE before TECHNICAL DETAIL, and the stack appears only under TECHNICAL DETAIL. The third failure says the worker is restarting now, not that it will try again.
- [ ] The 2026-11-17 input (`previous tick still running`, failure 2 of 3, tick started 10:19 UTC) yields the subject `[pit wall] Worker still stalled (2 of 3)` and a text that says it is the same stall as the earlier email.
- [ ] A failed job is named in words ("the daily numbers refresh for round 23") with the job id and error under TECHNICAL DETAIL; stale data gives the age in words and copes with nothing published.
- [ ] An all-clear is owed (`allClearSince`, pure, pinned) when this process sent a failure email and has had no good tick since, however short the trouble; otherwise when the previous heartbeat is older than the limit: two tick pauses (10 min) on a process's first tick, so three fast failures and a restart are covered, and the tick deadline plus one pause (30 min) after that, so a long job is not an outage. A deploy restart or an ordinary tick sends nothing.
- [ ] `serve.ts` sends the "Worker is back (was away …)" email under the 30-second alert deadline, only after the new heartbeat is written (a failed write must not repeat it every tick), never on `--once`, timed by the clock rather than the tick's start, and writes no `pw_alerts` row for it. The previous heartbeat is read before the stale check, so a failed stale check does not cost the all-clear.
- [ ] The `pw_alerts` record (subject, body) is unchanged, so the ops check's output does not change.
- [ ] Times are UTC; with `ALERT_TZ` set (an IANA zone) they read `04:49 CST (10:49 UTC)`; an unknown zone falls back to UTC.
- [ ] `npm test` in `workers/pitwall` exits 0.

## Targets
- binary (the worker on forge)

## Data
No user data. The emails carry times, round numbers, job ids and error text, to the owner's own address (`ALERT_TO`) through Resend, as before.

## Performance budget
n/a — string building on the failure path; the all-clear reuses the `pw_status/worker` read the tick already makes.

## Out of scope
- The hourly ops check's emails (`scripts/ops/pitwall-alert.js`, F-140).
- Why the box dropped off the network.
- Collapsing the three stall emails of one outage into one.
- Retrying an all-clear whose own send failed, and an all-clear after three fast failures when the restart also loses the heartbeat read.
- The attempt number in a failed-job email (`RunRecord` does not carry it); the text states the limit instead.
