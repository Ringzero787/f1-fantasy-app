# F-098 — UC: the ace freezes for every session it scores in, not just the race

Created: 2026-10-04 · Merged 2026-10-04 (PR #160) · Status: implemented · Follows [F-095](F-095.md), which closed the race half.

## The gap
Undercut (the Firestore rules, the lock sweep, the app and the Pit Wall portal).

The ace doubles a pick's points, and **three** sessions score with it applied:

| session | scored by | ace doubling |
|---|---|---|
| qualifying | `checkQualifyingResults`, every 10 min once the session ends | `calculatePoints.ts:318` |
| sprint | `checkSprintResults`, every 10 min once the session ends | `calculatePoints.ts:484` |
| race | `onRaceCompleted` | `calculatePoints.ts:815` |

Each reads `team.aceDriverId` **live** when it runs. F-095 froze the ace from lights out, which
closed the race and left the other two open: watch Q3, set your ace to the pole-sitter before the
qualifying scorer next runs, and those points double. On the six sprint rounds you could watch the
entire sprint and do the same — a far longer window than qualifying, and the one that prompted
this.

It is the same exploit as F-095 and needs no more than the web SDK and the player's own
credentials.

## Platforms and data

**Platforms:** Firestore security rules; Cloud Functions (`autoLockTeams`, `autoUnlockTeams`,
`createTeamSecure`); the Expo app (Android, iOS, Amazon — one JS bundle); the Pit Wall portal. No
native code, manifest, entitlement or build flag, so no store declaration changes.

**Data touched:** three more fields beside F-095's pair, all on
`fantasyTeams/<id>.lockStatus` — **`aceFreezeFrom`** (a Timestamp: the weekend's first ace-scoring
session), **`aceQualiKey`** and **`aceSprintKey`** (strings: `quali_<raceId>` and
`sprint_<raceId>`, the markers the session scorers add to the team's `scoredRaces`; the sprint one
only on sprint weekends). Written only by the lock sweep, cleared by the unlock sweep, readable by
whoever can already read the team. Session times and a race id — public information, no personal
data, nothing needing retention or export treatment. Absent or null on teams written before this
change; both cases fall back to F-095's behaviour rather than to "unlocked", so no backfill is
needed and the 75 teams the F-095 backfill stamped for Bahrain keep exactly the protection they
have.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The ace can be changed freely until the weekend's first ace-scoring session begins —
   qualifying, or the sprint on a sprint weekend.
2. From that moment the ace cannot be changed, through qualifying and through the sprint.
3. Once qualifying has been **scored** — and the sprint too, on a weekend that has one — the ace
   can be changed again, right up to lights out. That gap is the feature and it must open on the
   scoring, not on a clock.
4. From lights out the ace cannot be changed again until the team unlocks (F-095, unchanged).
5. When a session cannot be scored on the day — an unmapped driver leaves qualifying, or the
   sprint results, to be folded in at race time — the gap does not open, because the ace is still
   live for those points.
6. No team can end up permanently unable to change its ace, and nothing a client can call opens
   the freeze early.
7. Teams stamped by F-095, and teams written before either change, keep working with no backfill.

## What was built

**The freeze runs from the first scoring session to the failsafe ceiling, with one gap.**

1. **`functions/src/utils/lockTime.ts`** — `aceFreezeStart(race)`: the earlier of `schedule.sprint`
   (sprint weekends only) and `schedule.qualifying`, falling back to the race. Sprint qualifying is
   deliberately not in it: nothing scores sprint qualifying. It is where the *roster* locks, for
   the related reason that the sprint follows it, and the two locks stay distinct.
2. **`functions/src/locks/teamLocks.ts`** — `autoLockTeams` stamps `aceFreezeFrom`,
   `aceQualiKey` and (on sprint weekends) `aceSprintKey` alongside F-095's `aceLockTime` /
   `aceLockUntil`; `autoUnlockTeams` clears all five. The sprint marker is stamped on
   `schedule.sprint || hasSprint`, which fails closed: a sprint round whose OpenF1 sessions are
   not published yet carries the flag from seed data but no time, and stamping nothing there
   would let the gap open on qualifying alone — the hole, not the fix. `aceFreezeStart` fails
   closed on the same condition: with the flag set and no sprint time it starts the freeze at
   sprint qualifying, which precedes the sprint, rather than at qualifying hours after it.
3. **`firestore.rules`** — `aceIsFrozen` now runs from `aceFreezeFrom` (falling back to
   `aceLockTime`, which is what makes F-095-stamped teams behave exactly as they did) and opens
   the gap when `scoredRaces` carries `aceQualiKey`, plus `aceSprintKey` where there is one, and
   the race has not started. **The fallback is a type test, not a `get()` default**: in rules a
   default applies only when the key is *absent*, and `createTeamSecure` and the rescue script
   both write a present null — reading that as "no freeze" would have left every team either of
   them touched completely open, worse than before this feature existed. The function sits at
   nine `let` bindings; Firestore refuses to compile at eleven, which is why its conditions are
   packed rather than named one per line.
4. **`src/utils/lockout.ts`** and **`web/pitwall/src/data/team.ts`** — the same predicate, now
   taking the team rather than just its `lockStatus`, because it needs `scoredRaces`.
5. **`calculatePoints.ts`** — the qualifying and sprint scorers write for **every** team they
   walk, not only the ones that scored. The write was inside an `if (teamPoints !== 0)`, so a
   blank session meant the gap never opened for that player, the idempotency guard that reads the
   same marker never fired for them, and their roster-as-fielded snapshot was never recorded.
   The first attempt at this put the write in an `else` and took `pointsUpdates.push` with it —
   which left `affectedLeagues` containing only leagues that held a zero-scoring team, so
   qualifying and sprint standings would have gone stale until Sunday. The condition is gone
   rather than inverted, and `functions/test/sessionScoringShape.test.js` pins both halves.
6. Copy: the `complex` screen's "Ace selection still open until race start" banner asks
   `canChangeAce` rather than the calendar alone, and `canChangeAce` no longer ANDs
   `lockStatus.canModify` — that is false from the roster lock onwards, so the screen could never
   reach the designed gap at all. The store's three refusals now say "while a scoring session is
   running" rather than "once the race starts", which is no longer when they fire.

### Why the sprint is keyed off its time, not `hasSprint`
`syncSchedule` calls `schedule.sprint` the definitive marker and only ever flips `hasSprint` to
true, so a document carrying a sprint time with a stale flag would have frozen from qualifying —
hours *after* the sprint it has to cover. A freeze that starts too late is the whole bug.

### Why the gap keys off `scoredRaces` and not a clock
A guessed duration — "qualifying plus four hours" — reopens the ace before the points it would
change are banked whenever scoring runs late, which is precisely when it matters. `scoredRaces`
already carries `quali_<raceId>` the moment the qualifying scorer finishes with that team, and it
is on the rules' denied-key list, so the client cannot claim it early. The 24-hour ceiling is
still there underneath as the thing that makes the freeze self-expiring.

It also gets case 5 right for free. When a session cannot be scored on the day its marker never
arrives, the gap never opens, and the ace stays frozen — which is correct, because that session is
then folded into race scoring from a live read. Both markers are required for the same reason: the
sprint is exactly as able to miss its Saturday scoring run as qualifying is.

### What got stricter, deliberately
You can no longer move your ace **during** qualifying or **during** the sprint. The headline
window — choose your ace on the strength of qualifying, right up to lights out — is untouched.
Between the sprint being scored and qualifying starting, the ace is also frozen, which is more
than strictly necessary — on the 2026 sprint weekends that is roughly three to four hours of a
Saturday. Keeping one gap rather than three is a rule a player can hold in their head, and the
alternative is three independent windows in a security rule that already sits one binding under
the compiler's limit.

## Tests
- `rules-tests/firestore.rules.test.js` — sixteen cases in all. Six **fail against the rule they
  replace and pass against this one**: frozen through qualifying, frozen through the sprint,
  another race's marker does not open the gap, an unscored qualifying never opens it, a sprint
  weekend needs its sprint settled, and an explicit-null freeze start falls back rather than
  unfreezing. The rest are F-095's guarantees, several rewritten onto the new fixture, including
  the case that pins what the Bahrain backfill left on 75 live teams.
- `__tests__/store/aceLockoutCalendar.test.ts` — frozen mid-qualifying while the calendar still
  reports the ace free, free again once the key lands, frozen through the sprint. The first and
  third **fail without the session freeze**.
- `__tests__/utils/lockout.test.ts`, `web/pitwall/src/data/team.test.ts` — both predicates across
  every boundary, the fallback, the sprint marker, the half-written window, a non-list
  `scoredRaces`, and every shape a Timestamp arrives in.
- `functions/test/sessionScoringShape.test.js` — that neither session handler gates its team
  write on `teamPoints !== 0`, and that neither pushes to `pointsUpdates` from inside a branch.
  A shape test, in the spirit of `__tests__/store/iapWiring.test.ts`, because exercising these
  handlers needs a Firestore fake this repo does not have — and both halves were broken here
  within a day of each other, in opposite directions, without failing anything.
- `__tests__/simple/aceGateShape.test.ts` — that neither team screen's ace gate requires
  `canModify`, that both consult `serverAceLocked`, and that neither carries it as an unused
  import. Both of those shipped wrong here. Rendering either screen in jest needs expo-router
  and a dozen stores, which is why neither has a component test; this is the cheaper guard, and
  it fails against both mistakes.
- `functions/test/aceFreezeStart.test.js` — the session choice itself: qualifying on a normal
  weekend, the sprint on a sprint weekend, the sprint recognised by its time rather than the
  flag, a sprint scheduled after qualifying, partial schedules, and that the ace freeze and the
  roster lock remain different moments. **F-095's spec claimed this repo has no Cloud Functions
  test harness. It does** — `functions/test/*.test.js`, run by CI's G03 — and that claim was
  wrong.

## Deploy order
**Functions then rules**, as with F-095, and either order is safe: the new clause is inert until
something stamps `aceFreezeFrom`, and a stamp the old rules do not read is harmless. The pair has
to be in place before the next qualifying — Singapore, 2026-10-11, **which is a sprint round**.

**Installed builds will be behind, and that is visible.** 2.4.2 is in the stores with no knowledge
of any of this: it offers an ace change through qualifying and the sprint, the write is now
refused, and `syncTeamToFirebase` only logs the refusal — so the ace appears to move and then
quietly does not. That is the offer-then-refuse this feature exists to remove, inverted. It argues
for an app release soon after the deploy; until then the server is correct and the client is
optimistic, which is the right way round for competitive integrity but the wrong way round for
the player looking at it.

**The clients learn the gap has opened on their next read of the team, not the instant the scorer
writes.** Neither holds an `onSnapshot` on `fantasyTeams`. The rules are exact; the UI is a
refresh behind.

The Bahrain weekend of 2026-10-04 is **not** re-stamped. Its qualifying was scored before any of
this existed, so the gap would be open and the behaviour identical to what F-095 already gives it.

## Still open
Carried over from F-095 and not addressed here: `settings.lockDeadline: 'race'` leagues are never
swept; a team created after the sweep is never locked at all; `recalculateAllTeamPoints` and
`triggerPointsCalculation` re-score history with the *current* ace; server-side driver/constructor
ace exclusivity is unenforced. (F-095's claim that this repo has no Cloud Functions test harness
was wrong — `functions/test/*.test.js` exists and CI runs it — so `aceFreezeStart` has real tests
now. The sweep itself still has none: it needs a Firestore fake, which is its own piece of work.)

New here: making the session write unconditional widens a **pre-existing** lost-update window.
Both handlers read every team from one collection snapshot and later write `drivers` back
wholesale — which is why `totalPoints` uses `increment()` — and that clobber now covers every
team rather than only the scoring ones. It can only bite a team editable during a scoring run,
which means the two cases already listed above (a `lockDeadline: 'race'` league, or a team
created after the sweep). The fix is a transaction that re-reads the roster, or omitting
`drivers` when no entity's points changed. The same change makes `affectedLeagues` cover every
league rather than the scoring ones; at tens of teams that is nothing, but it scales with every
team ever created, inside a 120-second scheduled function whose caller does not retry.

Also new: a team whose per-session points cancel to exactly zero now gets a `raceSnapshots`
document like everyone else, where before it got none. That is the fix rather than a side
effect, but it does mean the snapshot collection grows by the teams that used to be skipped.

The freeze still ends on a wall clock (race + 24h) rather than on the race being scored,
so results more than a day late would see it expire — the same exposure, and the same reasoning,
as the roster failsafe it borrows the ceiling from.
