# F-083 — UC: Share league standings as text

Created: 2026-10-02 · Status: building

## Goal
Undercut (root app). At the end of a race weekend a league wants the whole table in one message: members to argue about moves, the commissioner to call people out by name. There is no way to get the standings out of the app today, so people screenshot a scrolling list and send three pictures of it.

A SHARE control on the League tab hands the table to the operating system's share sheet as text. Text rather than an image was a deliberate choice over a rendered card: an image needs `react-native-view-shot` and `expo-sharing`, two native modules, in an app that went live on three stores the same week, and a server-rendered image means putting member names on a URL. Text pastes into Slack, a text message and an email alike and needs nothing native behind it.

## Acceptance criteria
- [x] A SHARE control sits beside the view selector on the League tab and is absent when there are no standings, so it never opens a share sheet onto nothing.
- [x] It shares what is on screen. A league looking at a single round shares that round, and the heading names which view it is.
- [x] The message leads with the league name and the view, then one line per member: rank, name, score, and movement.
- [x] **No line exceeds 34 display columns**, heading included. A wrapped line reads as two rows and the table stops being a table. The budget is measured in display columns rather than characters: a CJK character occupies two monospace columns, and a display name is whatever Google or Apple hands over with no ASCII restriction anywhere in the sign-in path.
- [x] Truncation walks code points, so an emoji or a CJK character is never split into an invalid string.
- [x] The heading is uppercased before it is measured, because ß becomes SS and ﬁ becomes FI.
- [x] At most 20 rows, then a `+N more` line, so the message is one screen rather than a scroll.
- [x] No code fence. Backticks give Slack perfect monospace and give SMS and email three literal backticks; two of the three destinations would be worse off, so the layout degrades instead — every line starts with a rank and ends with a score, which reads in order in a proportional font.
- [x] Movement appears only for members who moved. A column of "—" belongs on screen and is noise in a message.
- [x] The formatter is pure and unit-tested without a React Native runtime, including CJK, emoji, halfwidth kana, combining accents and German case expansion.

## Data
**This feature moves other people's personal data off the device**, which is the whole point of it and needs saying plainly.

The shared text contains, for up to 20 people in the sharer's own league: **display name, rank, score and rank movement**. It does **not** contain user ids, team names, or anything about anyone outside that league. A sharer cannot emit data about a league they are not in.

Two things the first draft of this section got wrong, found by the privacy read:

- **A display name can be an email address in disguise.** An account created with email and password takes `email.split('@')[0]` as its display name (`src/services/auth.service.ts:49`), so a share can carry `nathan.shanks` — the local part of an address, and often enough to reconstruct it. Saying this section contains no email addresses was not true. The real fix is to stop deriving a display name from the address; until then it is stated here rather than glossed.
- **A past round can name people who have left.** Selecting a round reads a stored snapshot of that round, and unlike the live table it does not filter members who withdrew. The deletion cascade (`functions/src/users/deleteUserData.ts`) clears `leagues/{id}/members/{uid}` but not `leagues/{id}/raceResults/*`, where the name is a frozen copy. So sharing an old round can republish the name of someone who left the league, or deleted their account and has no erasure path. Pre-existing retention gap; this feature is what moves it off the device. Tracked below.

- The display name otherwise defaults to the Google or Apple account name, which is often a real name. It is editable in Profile.
- The share is always started by a person, never automatically, and the destination is chosen by that person in the operating system's own share sheet. Nothing is sent to our servers, and the app makes no network call.
- The other members do not consent to the specific destination. That is the same exposure as the screenshot this replaces, and the reason the data is kept to the minimum above rather than including everything on screen.
- No change to the Play Data Safety or App Store privacy declarations: the app neither collects nor transmits this data. The operating system hands it to an app the user picked.

## Performance budget
No new dependency. About 220 lines of pure TypeScript, so the bundle is effectively flat. The formatter runs once per share on at most 20 rows.

## Out of scope
- A rendered image card. Reconsider if the native-module cost ever looks cheap, or if a server-rendered card can avoid putting names on a URL.
- Sharing from anywhere but the League tab.
- Choosing how many rows to share, or which ones.

## Known gaps
- **Not localized.** The four new strings (`SHARE`, the accessibility label and hint, and the `+N more` line) are English. `src/simple/**` is not yet in `localization.scan`, so G16 cannot see them, and converting one control in an otherwise unconverted screen would be inconsistent. They belong to the F-079 pass over `src/simple/**`.
- **`+N more` has no plural category.** It is built by concatenation, which Polish and Japanese catalogs cannot express. Converting it under F-079 means a plural group, not a string.
- **A deleted account's name survives in round snapshots.** `deleteUserData` does not scrub `leagues/{id}/raceResults/*`, so an erased user remains named in every round they played, on screen and now in a shared message. This needs its own change to the deletion cascade, which fixes the screen as well as the share — a filter here would only hide it from one of the two.
- **No test covers the panel wiring** — that the control hides on an empty table and passes the on-screen selector. The formatter is covered; the component is not, and this codebase has no component-test setup.
