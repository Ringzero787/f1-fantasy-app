# F-102 — UC: the portal says when the ace freezes on a sprint weekend

Created: 2026-10-05 · Status: building · Follows [F-098](F-098.md).

## Why
F-098 moved the ace freeze to the weekend's first scoring session. On a normal weekend that is
qualifying, which is also when the server locks the roster, so nothing visible changed. **On a
sprint weekend the two come apart:** the roster locks at sprint qualifying on Friday and the ace
survives until Saturday's sprint — about twenty hours later.

The Pit Wall header says `Lineups locked` and nothing else, which reads as though the whole team
is settled. A player who believes that gives up a window the game is deliberately giving them.
Singapore (R19, 2026-10-11) is the first sprint round since F-098 shipped.

`pw_public/2026_19.round` carries `firstSession` and `locksIn` and has no sprint field at all, so
the published payload could not answer this either.

## Platforms and data
**Platforms:** the Pit Wall portal only. No functions, no rules, no app, no worker — and
therefore **no payload change and no worker deploy**: `loadAccount` already reads the whole
`races.schedule` client-side for `locksIn`, so the sprint and qualifying times are in hand.

**Data touched:** none. Nothing is written; two derived strings are added to the in-memory
`Account`.

## Acceptance criteria
1. On a sprint weekend, while the ace freeze is still ahead, the header names it — a countdown and
   the moment itself.
2. On a normal weekend the header says nothing extra, because the ace freeze and the roster lock
   are the same moment on the server.
3. Once the freeze has passed, the line goes away rather than counting up or reading `LOCKED`
   twice.
4. The portal's idea of when the ace freezes matches `aceFreezeStart` in
   `functions/src/utils/lockTime.ts`, including its fallbacks. If they disagree the portal is
   promising something the rules will not honour.

## What was built
- **`web/pitwall/src/lib/lock.ts`** — `aceFreezeTime(schedule, hasSprint)` mirroring the server,
  `sessionMoment` for the absolute label, and `aceOutlivesLineup` to decide whether there is
  anything to say.
- **`web/pitwall/src/lib/account.ts`** — `aceFreezesAtMs`, null unless the ace outlives the roster
  lock. A moment, not a formatted countdown: `loadAccount` runs once per sign-in and nothing ticks,
  so a baked-in countdown is a snapshot that ages, and a tab left open past the sprint would still
  be promising a window that had shut. `aceFreezeLine(atMs, now)` derives both halves at render
  time and returns null once the moment has passed, which is also what keeps criterion 3 true
  without a timer.
- **`web/pitwall/src/ui/Shell.tsx`** — one `.warn` span beside the lineup line, with the
  explanation on hover. The **moment is not hidden on a narrow screen** — it is the part worth
  reading, and a `title` tooltip is unreachable on touch, so only the countdown is `.mut`-dimmed
  rather than dropped. `--warn` was already defined in both themes and had no class; it does now,
  and the light-theme value moved from `#9A6B00` to `#7A5400` because the original was 4.26:1 on
  the page ground — below AA for 11px text. This is the token's first use, so nothing else moved.

What it renders, against the real Singapore schedule:

| when | header |
|---|---|
| now (Mon) | `Locks in 3d 14h` · `Ace freezes in 4d 10h · Sat 10 Oct 09:00 UTC` |
| Fri, after the roster lock | `Lineups locked` · `Ace freezes in 20h 00m · Sat 10 Oct 09:00 UTC` |
| Sat, during the sprint | `Lineups locked` (no ace line) |
| a normal weekend | `Locks in 19h 30m` (no ace line) |

### A discrepancy this uncovered, not caused
Deciding whether the ace outlives the roster lock meant comparing against a roster lock — and the
portal and the server do not agree on when that is:

| | normal weekend | sprint weekend |
|---|---|---|
| `lockTime` (portal, displayed) | **FP3** | sprint qualifying |
| `effectiveLockTime` (server, enforced) | **qualifying** | sprint qualifying |

On a normal weekend the portal tells players lineups lock at FP3 while the server locks them at
qualifying, three and a half hours later. It predates this change and it is the safe direction —
the portal is early, so nobody is caught out — but it is wrong, and taking the portal's number
here would have shown the ace line on every weekend rather than sprint weekends. So
`aceOutlivesLineup` measures against a local `serverLockTime` that mirrors the enforced rule, and
`aceFreeze.test.ts` pins the discrepancy so it is visible rather than folklore.

**Not fixed here.** Correcting the displayed lock would change what players plan around, mid-season,
in a week where the ace timing has already moved. It wants its own change and the owner's word.

## Tests
`web/pitwall/src/lib/aceFreeze.test.ts` — sixteen cases over the real Singapore and Bahrain
schedules:
the freeze is the sprint where there is one and qualifying otherwise; it is read off
`schedule.sprint` rather than the `hasSprint` flag; a sprint round with no sprint time yet falls
back to sprint qualifying, which precedes the sprint, rather than past it; partial schedules fall
back rather than returning nothing; and the line shows on a sprint weekend and not on a normal one.
Plus `aceFreezeLine`: it names the moment and counts down, it returns null once the freeze has
passed rather than counting up or reading `LOCKED` twice, and the same stored moment read at three
different times gives three different answers — the test for the staleness the render-time
derivation exists to prevent.

They cover every case in `functions/test/aceFreezeStart.test.js` that applies to a schedule object
— including the sprint-after-qualifying ordering and a sprint round with neither sprint time —
which is what keeps criterion 4 honest. The server's test also exercises `effectiveLockTime`
parity, which has no portal equivalent beyond `serverLockTime` being a copy of it.

**The Singapore fixture is the live `races/singapore_2026` document, not `seedData.ts`.** The seed
has the sprint an hour later and the OpenF1 sync supersedes it; a review read the seed and flagged
the fixture as wrong. The test says so, so the next reader does not "correct" it.

## Still open
- **The FP3-versus-qualifying discrepancy above.** Prose and a pinned test, with no feature id —
  it needs one if it is ever to be fixed rather than rediscovered.
- **Leagues with `settings.lockDeadline: 'race'` never get an ace window at all**, because the
  sweep's stamp sits inside its `=== 'qualifying'` branch (`functions/src/locks/teamLocks.ts`).
  Carried from F-098's own list; the header will simply say nothing for such a team, which is the
  safe direction but not an accurate one. No such league exists today.
- **The countdown advances on re-render, not on a clock.** Any interaction refreshes it and the
  line cannot outlive the moment, but a page sitting untouched will show a stale *number* beside
  an always-correct *moment*. `locksIn` beside it has the same property and predates this.
- The published payload still carries no sprint field, so a reader who is not signed in — the
  example payload, or someone without a team — gets no ace line at all. It needs `hasSprint` and
  the session times in `pw_public.round`, which is a worker change.
